Why a specialist

Not a subject tutor: a study skills specialist

A subject tutor focuses on what is taught. I focus on how your teenager takes information in and remembers it. If revision isn't working, more practice of the same methods usually leads to burnout, not better marks. Changing how they study is what makes the content stick, whatever the subject.

  • Knowing how they learn. We work out which ways of studying suit your teenager, so they can choose the right strategy for each task rather than just working harder. Specialists call this metacognition.
  • Taking the load off memory. Dyslexia often affects working memory and how quickly words and sounds are processed. Rereading pages of notes plays to those weaknesses. Instead, we use structure, such as visual plans, templates and mind maps, and technology, such as text-to-speech and dictation, so less effort goes on the mechanics.
  • Energy for the thinking that earns marks. With the mechanics handled, more energy goes into understanding, analysing and evaluating, which is what higher marks reward.

I hold a Level 5 Diploma in Specialist Teaching for Literacy Difficulties, I have worked as a specialist study skills tutor for college and university students with specific learning differences, and I am dyslexic myself.

Getting started

What we can work on together

We start with what your teenager finds difficult to revise and why, which strategies are already working well, and how to prioritise specific subjects. We also explore which ways of learning work best for them, so strategies are tailored to suit them.

Areas covered

  • Creating a realistic timetable
  • Planning revision
  • Remembering information
  • Making useful notes
  • Understanding assignment briefs and exam questions
  • Planning answers
  • Using assistive technology

How sessions work

Starting from what your teenager needs

We begin by talking about what feels difficult, what already helps and what your teenager would like to achieve. Sessions use their own school materials, so the strategies connect with the work they need to do.

The aim is for your teenager to leave with practical approaches they can use between sessions, with opportunities to review and adapt them together.

FAQ

More questions

Is this subject tuition?

The focus is on how to study and organise learning. Sessions complement the subject teaching your teenager receives at school.

Does my teenager need a dyslexia diagnosis?

Support from me does not depend on a diagnosis. Sessions are open to dyslexic students, those with literacy difficulties, or young people who simply find revision challenging.

Where do sessions take place?

Sessions are in person: at school, or after school at home, or on weekend mornings.
