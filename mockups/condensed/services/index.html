Specialist teaching

What is a dyslexia specialist?
  • To practise at this level, I have a Diploma in Specialist Teaching for Literacy Difficulties (Level 5).
  • I am a member of the Dyslexia Guild.
  • My training enables me to develop your child’s cognitive tools to navigate language independently.
  • I teach explicit metacognitive strategies so that they can self-correct independently.
  • I use a multi-sensory framework to rewire neural pathways.
  • I facilitate learning by carefully controlling lesson content, ensuring all elements positively reinforce what is taught.
  • I enable your child to assimilate the new learning taught each session by focusing on information that they have been explicitly taught and shown to be secure with before introducing new learning.

Learning Needs Assessment £300, or two instalments of £150 · ages 6–16

A Learning Needs Assessment (LNA) looks at the gap between where your child is now with their learning and where they need to be, so that support is based on evidence.

An LNA takes approximately two hours and enables me to hypothesise why your child might have difficulty with progress, attainment, or achievement. You receive a report detailing your child's strengths and areas for development and recommendations and strategies to make learning more accessible, within two weeks of the assessment. It does not offer a diagnosis.

Payment, cancellation and consent details are in the terms and conditions and the assessment booking and consent form (PDF).

Waiting for answers from school?

Waiting for answers from school about why your child isn't making progress? Educational psychology time is stretched, and it often runs out before every child who needs it has been seen. As a dyslexia specialist I can help you explore the difficulties your child is experiencing and identify their barriers to learning.

Is this a diagnostic dyslexia assessment?

A Learning Needs Assessment tells you where your child's strengths are, what is getting in the way of their learning, and what to do next. The written report sets out an analysis of the barriers, along with recommendations and strategies you can act on. Parents find it useful for conversations with school, and schools find it useful when they are planning support.

What it is not is a formal diagnosis of dyslexia. Full diagnostic dyslexia assessments are in preparation, and are not available yet. If that is what you are looking for, please get in touch and I will let you know as soon as they open.

In the meantime, there is plenty you can do. The assessment report can be shared with your child's school and used to shape the support they put in place. You may also find the local SEND support page helpful, which lists East Riding services for families, and you are welcome to get in touch for a free call to talk your situation through.

1-1 Literacy Intervention £50/hour · ages 7–16

Specialist 1-to-1 tuition (structured literacy intervention): evidence-based support for children experiencing literacy difficulties, in-person, hour-long sessions that are cumulative, individually tailored, structured and multi-sensory, using the DALP framework with children aged 7–16.

Getting started: The first session uses the DALP's Diagnostic Placement Screening to identify gaps in knowledge and create a targeted plan.

C. Bell, parent
What does a typical session look like?

By using structure, this enables the child to feel secure. Sessions are fast-paced, so your child won’t get bored or tired.

Outline of a typical session

  • Visual and auditory warm-ups.
  • Review of previous patterns (overlearning).
  • Introduction of one small, logical, new concept.
  • Structured reading and spelling practice using the new concept.
  • Metacognitive review: what did you use to figure that out?
  • A game.
What the DALP covers

The DALP framework focuses on phonemic awareness, coding subskills (syllable types and grapheme–phoneme correspondence), punctuation and syntax, suffixing, coding attack patterns, and metacognition, helping a learner understand how they think and learn.

FAQ

Common questions

How much does an assessment cost?

A Learning Needs Assessment is £300. That covers the two-hour assessment and the written report that follows it. It can be paid in two instalments of £150.

What ages do you work with?

Learning Needs Assessments are for children aged 6 to 16. Specialist 1-to-1 tuition, the structured literacy intervention, is for children and young people aged 7 to 16.

How long does an assessment take, and what do I get?

The assessment takes approximately two hours. You receive a report detailing your child's strengths and areas for development, an analysis of barriers to learning, and recommendations and strategies to make learning more accessible. You receive the written report within two weeks of the assessment.

Is a Learning Needs Assessment a diagnosis of dyslexia?

No, it does not offer a diagnosis. It identifies strengths, barriers to learning and what would help. Full diagnostic dyslexia assessments are in preparation, so please get in touch if you would like to be told when they open.

When and where do sessions happen?

Sessions are delivered in person, one to one, during the school day. I work within about 20 miles of Pocklington, including York, Market Weighton, Driffield and Beverley. The school day is already demanding for a dyslexic child, so I avoid adding sessions after school or at weekends. I work in partnership with you and your child's school to find a time that suits everyone.

If sessions in school aren't possible, for example if your child is home-educated or their school can't host sessions, please still get in touch. I can sometimes work at your home or in a local space, and we will find the best arrangement for your child.

Does my child need an assessment before tuition?

No. The first tuition session includes a screening that pinpoints exactly where your child's difficulties lie, so the programme can be tailored to them from the start.

How long does the intervention last?

It depends on the nature of your child's difficulties; however, progress is reviewed at regular intervals.

What is different about the DALP intervention versus school phonics lessons?

Systematic Synthetic Phonics instruction is not a universal fix. Research indicates that up to 25% of children, including most children with dyslexia and specific learning difficulties, cannot learn to read using phonics alone. Dyslexic learners benefit from multi-sensory approaches that incorporate morphological instruction that build sight word vocabulary.

How do I get started?

Book a free 20-minute call, by phone or video, and tell me what you are looking for. There is no obligation.
