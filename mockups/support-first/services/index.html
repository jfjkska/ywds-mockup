What the sessions are like

Sessions are structured, cumulative and multi-sensory, using the Dyslexia Action Literacy Programme (DALP). The same shape each week helps your child know what to expect, and I adapt the pace and the activities to how your child is responding on the day.

A typical session

  1. Visual and auditory warm-ups.
  2. Review of previous patterns (overlearning).
  3. One small, logical, new concept.
  4. Structured reading and spelling practice using the new concept.
  5. A review of how your child worked it out: "what did you use to figure that out?"
  6. A game.

The DALP covers phonemic awareness, syllable types and sound–letter patterns, punctuation and syntax, suffixing, patterns for tackling longer words, and metacognition, which means helping your child understand how they think and learn.

I plan each lesson carefully so that every part reinforces what has just been taught. New learning is introduced only once the earlier learning is secure. Your child is taught explicit strategies for checking their own work, so they can start to self-correct.

As a dyslexia specialist, I hold a Diploma in Specialist Teaching for Literacy Difficulties (Level 5) and I am a member of the Dyslexia Guild.

Optional

Learning Needs Assessment (LNA)

You do not need an LNA before support starts. It is there if you would like a fuller picture of how your child learns, or a written report to share with school.

What it is

  • For children aged 6–16.
  • A battery of standardised tests, taking approximately two hours, in school or at another agreed place.
  • A written report within two weeks, setting out your child's strengths and areas for development, the barriers to learning, and recommendations and strategies to make learning more accessible.
  • It gives an indication of whether dyslexia is likely, and can help to spot signs of other learning needs, which I can signpost you on.

What it is not

  • It is not a diagnosis of dyslexia.
  • It is not needed before 1-1 support begins.

Waiting for a diagnosis, or for answers from school?

You have choices, and none of them is wrong:

  • You don't need a diagnosis to get support. Schools should put support in place based on what your child needs, with or without a diagnosis. My support doesn't need one either.
  • Free signposting is available. The local SEND support page lists East Riding services for families, and you are welcome to book a free call to talk your situation through.
  • An LNA can sit alongside a later diagnosis. It gives you written evidence to share now. If you later have a diagnostic assessment with me within 12 months of the LNA, £75 (25% of the LNA fee) comes off its price.
  • If you decide to wait, that is a reasonable choice. The free guides and local support page may help in the meantime.
An LNA and a diagnostic assessment: how are they different?

An LNA tells you where your child's strengths are, what is getting in the way of their learning, and what to do next. A diagnostic assessment can offer an explanation, validation and practical recommendations. Ongoing sessions develop reading and spelling skills. They are different things that can sit alongside each other, and you can choose any of them, or none.

FAQ

More questions

What ages do you work with?

Learning Needs Assessments are for children aged 6 to 16. 1-1 literacy support is for children and young people aged 7 to 16.

How long does the support last?

It depends on the nature of your child's difficulties, so there is no fixed-length course to sign up to. Progress is reviewed regularly, and you pay monthly or one block of six sessions at a time, so you can decide at each stage.

How is this different from school phonics lessons?

School phonics teaching helps many children, including many dyslexic children, to learn to decode. Some children need more: extra practice, in small steps, with each step checked before the next. The DALP is structured, cumulative and multi-sensory, and covers spelling patterns, word parts such as suffixes, and building sight word vocabulary alongside phonics.

What if my child's school cannot host sessions?

Please still get in touch. I can sometimes work at your home or in a local space. If sessions are at home or another place, a parent, carer or other responsible adult you have named must be in the building for the whole session.
