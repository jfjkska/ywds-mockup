1Say

Set the agenda early.

At the start“I’m worried about reading and spelling. Could we focus on those tonight, please?”
If you hear “they’re settled in class”“At home, homework often ends in tears. Can we look at what’s making it so hard?”
+Bring one or two examples. A word read one day but not the next, or spelling homework that takes far longer than expected.
Both accounts matter.

2Ask

Questions to take with you.

  1. What can my child do confidently, and where do they need help?
  2. What reading or spelling support is already in place?
  3. What will we try next, and how will we know it’s helping?
  4. What would be useful to practise at home?
  5. When will we review this, and who do I contact meanwhile?
No need to ask them all.

Before you go, jot down:

What support will be triedIn class, in a group or one to one

Who will arrange itA name, not just “school”

When you’ll review itOut of time? Ask for a follow-up chat

You’re not being a difficult parent by asking for clarity.
